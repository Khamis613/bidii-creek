How it began

Bidii Creek Conservancy did not begin as an organization. It began as an individual effort, one person who refused to look away

Along the creek at Jomvu Kuu, the mangrove forest was disappearing. Trees were being cut down illegally, and stretches of shoreline that had once been green were left bare and degraded. Witnessing this loss, one member of the community decided to act, and began working to protect what was left of the forest and to bring back what had been lost.

That single effort became the seed of everything that followed. Bidii means hard work and determination in Kiswahili, and it is exactly what the years ahead would demand.

What began as one person's stand against the logging of our mangroves has grown into a community movement to restore the creek.

Our journey

The milestones, setbacks and breakthroughs that shaped Bidii Creek.

  1. 2013

    Community mobilisation

    The conservation effort grew beyond one person. A group of community members gathered around the same goal which was to stop the destruction and restore the forest.

    Together, they established the creek's first mangrove nursery, raising 500 seedlings by hand to replant the degraded shoreline.

  2. The hard years

    A setback

    Protecting the ecosystem without funding proved hard. With no financial support, many members could not continue, and the group shrank until only 8 dedicated people remained.

    They refused to give up. By this time, those few determined members had planted 4,500 mangrove trees, proof that the work was possible even with almost nothing.

  3. 2016

    Recognition

    The persistence paid off. In 2016, the conservancy gained recognition for its work, giving the group a formal identity and opening the door to new support.

    Between 2016 and 2019, the conservancy planted a further 15,000 trees along the creek.

  4. 2019

    Active sponsorship

    For the first time, organizations stepped in with active sponsorship of the conservancy's work. The support made a direct difference on the ground, funding the planting of an additional 10,000 trees.

  5. 2021

    A turning point

    By 2021, the conservancy had planted over 100,000 mangroves.

    The same year, Kenya Pipeline Company partnered with Bidii Creek. The partnership changed the scale of the work completely: 300,000 trees were planted, more than in all the years before it combined.

  6. 2022 – 2025

    Growing every year

    The momentum continued. Every year since, the conservancy has planted on a large scale with its partners and the community:

    • 2022200,000 trees
    • 2023150,000 trees
    • 2024150,000 trees
    • 2025200,000 trees
  7. Today

    A record

    5.6 million mangroves

    Today, Bidii Creek's conservation work has restored 250 hectares of degraded land. Approximately 2.5 million trees have been planted through community engagements and events, and the conservancy's current record stands at 5.6 million mangroves.

Beyond planting, A creek sustains its community

Restoring the mangroves is only part of the story. Bidii creek also supports the livelihoods of the people who protect it.

  • Fishing

    Mangrove roots are nurseries for fish. As the forest recovers, a safe breeding environment is restored.

  • Crab fattening

    Members raise mud crabs in the creek, an income that relies on keeping the mangrove habitat healthy.

  • Beekeeping

    Established 100 beehives to promote mangrove beekeeping, generating income while enhancing pollination and biodiversity. .

  • Community education

    To promote awareness and coastal protection, we teach schools and the wider community why mangroves matter and the benefits we get from their existence.
